Move

Selling and buying together

Three ways to order a South Bay move. Read all of them, then emphasize one. Oceans can help you compare. The choice is still yours.

Oceans Real Estate Group does not provide lending, legal, or tax advice, and does not guarantee a sale, a purchase, a date, or proceeds. Ask your lender, attorney, and tax advisor about your own situation.

Buy first

You shop while you still live in your current home, then sell after the next home is under contract or has closed.

Timeline
There can be a stretch when you own two homes. The length depends on both transactions, not on a schedule Oceans can promise.
Trade-off
You can choose the next home with less pressure to move out on a deadline. You carry the overlap.
Financing questions
Ask your lender whether you can qualify while you still own, and what cash the overlap would require. Oceans does not lend.
Temporary housing
Often unnecessary if you can close the purchase before you move out. If a date slips, you may need a short stay elsewhere. We do not arrange that stay.
Contingency choices
A sale-of-home contingency is something to discuss. Some sellers will not accept it. Nothing here promises that a seller will.

Sell first

You sell the current home, then shop. You learn the result of the sale before you commit to the purchase.

Timeline
There may be a gap between homes. How long that gap lasts is not something this page can predict.
Trade-off
You know your proceeds before you buy. You may move twice.
Financing questions
Ask your lender how a completed sale changes what you can buy, and what proof they will want. That answer is theirs, not ours.
Temporary housing
Plan for a rental, a stay with family, or a longer close if you need one. Oceans does not promise a vacancy or provide housing.
Contingency choices
Offers can be written with fewer home-sale conditions, which some sellers prefer. Acceptance is still the seller's decision.

Coordinated closing

The sale and the purchase are aimed at the same window, so one move is the goal.

Timeline
Both closings have to land near each other. If either date moves, the plan needs a backup.
Trade-off
One move, and less room if a lender, a buyer, or a seller changes the date.
Financing questions
Ask your lender what happens to the purchase if the sale closes later than expected, and what cash has to be in place that day.
Temporary housing
Keep a backup for a few nights even if you hope not to use it. A hotel or a relative's guest room is a plan, not a failure.
Contingency choices
Discuss which contract depends on the other. Have your own advisor read anything you are unsure about. This is not legal advice.
