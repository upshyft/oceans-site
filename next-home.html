How you buy the next house

The decision is the structure of the purchase, not a slogan about the coast. Cash, a loan, and equity that is still in another house produce different offers and different monthly costs.

Oceans Real Estate Group is not a lender, mortgage broker, or financial, tax, or legal adviser. We do not provide lending, legal, or tax advice, we do not recommend a product, a rate, or a lender, and Oceans does not guarantee approval, a sale, or a return.

Questions that change the offer

  1. Compare loan structures, not the headline rate alone. Ask each lender, on the same terms, for the rate, the points, whether the rate is fixed or can reset, the term, any prepayment cost, the cash due at closing, and the payment. A lower rate can still mean a higher payment if the loan is larger or shorter.
  2. Cash versus financed. A cash offer can remove a financing contingency and the appraisal a lender would require. A financed offer can leave your cash invested somewhere else. A seller may treat those offers differently. Which one you can carry is a lender question.
  3. If the next purchase depends on equity you do not have in hand yet, the timing tools are different: a sale contingency, a bridge, or a line against the house you still own. Ask the lender what each costs per month and what happens if the sale closes late. Those products are theirs, not ours.
  4. Appraisal and inspection risk. If an appraisal comes in under the price, a financed offer may need more cash or a new price. An inspection can reopen the terms. Decide before you write which of those you can absorb.
  5. If you will own two houses for a while, price the overlap: both loan payments, taxes, insurance, utilities, and any HOA dues. The move plan is the order-of-closings version of that question.
