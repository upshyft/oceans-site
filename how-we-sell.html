How we'd sell your home

A sample launch kit you can open section by section. It shows the kind of work a South Bay listing conversation covers. Every part below is an illustrative sample.

Illustrative sample. This is not a current listing, not a client, and not a sale result. Oceans is not promising a price, a number of showings, or a buyer.

Preparation priorities

In a real conversation we would walk the home with you and sort tasks into three piles: do now, decide later, and ask before spending. A sample order:

  1. Make the main rooms easy to walk through.
  2. Note repairs you already know about, without hiring anyone yet.
  3. Agree on what a photographer must be able to reach.
  4. Leave spending decisions for the "ask before spending" list on the seller prep board.

Photography brief

Sample brief only. It does not name a photographer, a vendor, or a real house.

  • Front of the home in open shade when that light is available.
  • The main living space with lamps on and small clutter off the counters.
  • The kitchen as it is used, not restaged into a room the owner does not recognize.
  • One quiet outdoor frame that shows a coastal setting without identifying an address.
  • No room is photographed until the owner says that room is ready.

Property-story example

Illustrative sample. Not a listing Oceans has taken, and not a client's home.

Sample story: a made-up one-story home a short walk from the beach, with a lived-in kitchen and a front path that gets afternoon light. The imaginary owner wants a buyer to notice the quiet street and a room used as an office. That is the shape of a property story. It is not a house for sale, and nothing in it happened.

Launch timeline

A sample sequence, not a promise of how many days a home will take.

  1. First conversation: walk the home, list prep, and mark anything that should wait for a cost question.
  2. Next: photography and a story draft the owner can correct.
  3. Launch week: publish when the owner says the home is ready, not on a date picked to sound fast.
  4. After launch: a written update of what happened, including if the honest update is that there were no showings yet.

Seller-update preview

Illustrative sample. These lines are blanks for the kind of note we would write. They are not activity from a real week.

  • Period: a sample week, not a week that occurred.
  • What we would record: who asked to see the home, in words, without turning it into a score.
  • Feedback themes we would pass on: light, storage, street noise — examples of topics, not quotes from buyers.
  • Next step we would discuss: whether showing hours still fit the owner's life.
  • What this sample refuses to invent: an offer, a sale, or a prediction.
