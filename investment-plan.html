Short-term, mid-term, or a lease

The decision is which rental you are actually willing to run. A short-term rental (STR) turns over in nights. A mid-term rental (MTR) is a furnished stay of roughly a month or more. A long-term rental (LTR) is a residential lease. None of the three is the right answer at every address.

Oceans Real Estate Group does not provide lending, legal, or tax advice, and does not guarantee that an STR, MTR, or LTR is allowed, or any rent, occupancy, or return. Rules, insurance, financing, taxes, and licenses are specific to the property and the jurisdiction. Verify them with the city, the HOA, your insurer, your lender, and your tax adviser. We are not a lender and we do not recommend a loan, a rate, or one strategy for every buyer.

How the strategy changes the job

Who stays

STR. Different guests, often for a few nights. You are running a hospitality turn, not a lease.

MTR. One furnished stay of about a month or longer. The guest sits between a hotel guest and a tenant.

LTR. One household on a lease. Fewer people through the door.

Furnishing and setup

STR. Furnished, stocked, and ready to reset. Linens, a working kitchen, and a clean between guests are part of the model.

MTR. Furnished as well, but the guest lives there long enough to need storage and a kitchen they can actually use, not only a weekend setup.

LTR. Often unfurnished. The tenant brings the household. You are not resetting the unit every few days.

Management

STR. Messages, pricing, cleaning, and problems between guests. That is you, or a manager you hire. This page is not an offer to manage it.

MTR. Fewer turns than nightly stays, and more contact than a year-long lease. The furniture still has to be maintained.

LTR. Leasing, collecting rent, and repairs. Less daily work, and a longer tie to one household.

Turnover and vacancy

STR. High turnover. A gap between guests is vacancy you feel right away.

MTR. The empty stretch is more likely a month than a night. Filling the next month is the risk.

LTR. Vacancy shows up when a lease ends. One empty month is a larger hole, and the unit may sit until the next lease.

Pricing

STR. The rate can change by the night. That cuts both ways. This page will not quote one.

MTR. Priced by the stay or the month. It can move with the length of the stay. Still not a number we will invent.

LTR. A lease rent that usually holds until renewal. The risk is the gap between tenants, not a nightly swing.

Costs and reserves

STR. Cleaning every turn, supplies, utilities you often pay, and replacement of furniture and linens. Reserve for vacant nights and for those replacements before you call the rest income.

MTR. Utilities and furnishing replacement still sit with you more often than on a lease. A vacant month needs a reserve of its own.

LTR. The tenant may pay some utilities. You still reserve for repairs and for time between leases. A roof or a kitchen is a separate check from the monthly leftover.

What you have to verify

STR. Whether stays of that length are allowed at this address, by the city and by the HOA. Whether the insurance and the loan allow that use. What license or tax registration that place requires. This page does not answer any of those.

MTR. The same stack. A stay of a month or more can still be limited by a city, an HOA, a policy, or a loan. Read the minimum and maximum stay. Do not assume it is treated as a lease.

LTR. Lease rules, any HOA rental cap or waiting period, and whether the insurance and the loan assume an owner-occupant. Read HOA reserves and any special assessment before you treat dues as a small fixed cost.

Getting out

STR. Furniture can be removed, and you are not usually bound to one guest. A later buyer or lender may still underwrite the property as a rental, or may not.

MTR. A guest may be in place for the rest of a stay. The furniture is part of what you own.

LTR. A lease can run past the date you want to sell or move in. If you need that read as a legal one, it comes from your attorney. A tax-deferred exchange, if you are considering one, is a question for your tax adviser. We do not design it.

Questions to answer before choosing

  1. Is this length of stay allowed at this address, by the city and by the HOA, or are you guessing?
  2. Will the insurance policy and the loan allow that use, or do they require a different policy or a different loan?
  3. Who does the turns, and what does a vacant stretch cost if bookings or the next tenant are slow?
  4. What has to be in the unit before the first stay or the first lease, and who replaces it when it wears out?
  5. If you later want a different strategy, or a sale, what is stuck: a lease, the furniture, or a loan that assumed one use?
